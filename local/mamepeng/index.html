    Every jar tastes of where the bees flew.

    Our bees forage up to five kilometres from the hive. The flowers inside that ring decide the colour, the thickness and the taste. Change the landscape.

    MultifloraMany flowers in one jar. Amber, rounded and a little different every harvest. This is the honey in our Mamepeng jar.

    Two or three harvests a year

    And the bees bring you the jar.

    1. Check every hive twice a week for wasps, spiders and wax moth.
    2. Wait for capped comb. Bees seal ripe honey in wax, and we check the moisture.
    3. Uncap the frames and keep the wax for candles.
    4. Spin the honey out in our extractor.
    5. Sieve out wax and comb.
    6. Barely warm it, at a very low heat that keeps the nutrients in.
    7. Weigh and bottle every jar by hand.

    How to spot real honey

    Put it in the fridge.

    Real raw honey crystallises in the cold. Honey mixed with syrup stays thin and runny however cold it gets. Try it on our jar.

    Golden and pourable at room temperature. Slide to cool it down.
