Local script

Permission message first, then their preview site, then the call. Every business on your list has a preview website built for them, so the site does the selling. Your job: get them to look, find out what would make it a yes, and send the pay link.

The rhythm: four touches, then leave them alone

Day 0 permission message, then their site once they say yes · Day 1 follow-up · Day 4 follow-up · Day 11 last follow-up. The desk shows "Follow-up due" and puts the right message on button 3. After touch 4, stop. No reply means no: log "No reply on WhatsApp" and move on. Benchmark: 50 dials a day.

Opened their site (orange badge) means they looked in the last week. Those go to the top: call them now.

1 · Permission message (WhatsApp)

Hi, is this [Business]? I'm [your name] from Digital Switch. We've built a preview of a website for [Business], so you can see exactly what it would look like before paying anything. May I send you the link?

Use the 1 · Permission message button. When they say yes, press 2 · Send their site and log Site sent.

2 · The call (about 5 minutes)

  1. Open: "Hi, it's [your name] from Digital Switch. I sent you the website we built for [Business]. Did you get a chance to look?"
  2. If not: "Have you got one minute to open it on your phone now? I'll wait."
  3. Ask: "What did you think? What would you change before you'd put it in front of customers?" Then listen and log it in their words.
  4. Find the pain: "Where do most of your customers find you at the moment?" and "What happens when someone Googles you?"
  5. Show the 24/7 customer catcher: "When someone finds you on Google at 10 at night, they can WhatsApp or call you straight from the site, or leave their details. Nobody is lost while you're busy on a job."
  6. The offer: packages below. Lead with Starter unless they sell products or take bookings.
  7. Handle the objection (below), then ask again.
  8. Close: "Shall I send you the payment link so we can have it live this week?"
  9. Send the pay link with the Closing kit button, log Ready to pay, then Sold once they've paid.

The two packages

Starter, R3,999 once-off, then R99 a month. A landing website of up to 3 pages on your own web address, set up on Google, with tap-to-call, WhatsApp and a contact form. Live within 24 hours, plus a monthly report of visitors and everyone who contacted you.
Premium, R7,999 once-off, then R199 a month. A full website where customers can buy or book online, with everything in Starter.

No contract on the monthly. Payflex lets them pay in 4 instalments, and DS is still paid upfront. You never take money: the client pays Digital Switch directly through the pay link. No discounts unless Lethabo approves in writing.

Upsell to Premium when…

They sell products, take bookings or appointments, or ask "can people order from it?" Say: "Then you'd want Premium: customers can book or buy straight from the site, even at night."

Handling objections: LEAR

An objection is a question in disguise. Never argue: you're on their side. Use LEAR every time, and say the lines in your own words.

  1. Listen. Let them finish. Don't interrupt and don't rush to answer. Listen for what is really behind it.
  2. Empathise. Show you understand: "I hear you", "That makes sense", "Fair question". Say "and", never "but".
  3. Ask. One open question to find the real reason, like "How much is one new customer worth to you?" Their answer does the selling.
  4. Respond. Answer the real concern briefly and honestly, then check: "Does that help?" and ask for the next step.

Two rounds of LEAR on the same objection, then let it go kindly. A "no" today is often a "yes" on touch 3. Never say "honestly", "trust me" or "you're wrong", and never promise Google rankings or a number of customers.

Money

"It's too expensive."

Listen for Too expensive compared to what? Usually they can't yet see what it's worth to them, or the cash isn't there this month.

Empathise "I hear you. R3,999 is real money for a small business, and you work hard for it."

Ask "Can I ask: how much is one new customer worth to you, roughly? And if the site brought you one or two extra customers a month, would that cover it?"

Respond "Most small business websites in South Africa cost R7,500 to R20,000. Yours is already built, so it's R3,999 once-off, and with Payflex you can split that into four payments of about R1,000. After that it's R99 a month, no contract. So one or two customers would cover it."

If the price is still the issue, ask "Is it the total, or the timing?" A timing problem gets a call-back on a date they choose. Tag Price.

"I don't have the money right now."

Listen for Cash flow, not value. Often true near month-end.

Empathise "That makes sense. Cash is tight for lots of businesses at the moment, especially before month-end."
